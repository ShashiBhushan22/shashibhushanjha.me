Quantum Communication & Quantum-Safe Networks

My primary doctoral research interest is quantum communication and its intersection with information technology. I am building a rigorous foundation in quantum information representation, physical channel modeling, quantum key distribution, repeaters and network protocols, and the integration of post-quantum cryptography with quantum-secure infrastructure. This direction lets me extend my communication-theory and simulation background while learning the required quantum mechanics carefully.

Technical foundation

Mathematics and systems under study

Quantum information representation

  • Complex Hilbert spaces, bra–ket notation, tensor products, and Hermitian or unitary operators
  • Pure states, mixed states, density matrices, reduced states, the Bloch sphere, and partial trace
  • Qubits, qudits, continuous-variable states, and composite or entangled systems
  • Photonic encodings in polarization, phase, time bins, and frequency bins, together with interfaces to matter-based quantum memories
  • The Born rule, projective measurement, POVMs, state discrimination, and quantum tomography
  • Shannon and von Neumann entropy, mutual information, relative entropy, and Holevo information

Communication, channels, and security

  • CPTP maps and Kraus, Choi, and Stinespring representations of quantum channels
  • Pure-loss and thermal-loss bosonic channels, plus dephasing, depolarizing, erasure, and amplitude-damping models
  • QKD protocol families and techniques, including BB84 and E91, decoy-state methods, measurement-device-independent and twin-field QKD, and continuous-variable QKD
  • QBER, secret-key rate, reconciliation, privacy amplification, composable security, and finite-key analysis
  • Fiber, terrestrial free-space, and satellite optical links; sources, detectors, loss, dark counts, and side channels
  • Quantum repeaters, memories, error correction, entanglement distribution, routing, and classical control

Research landscape

Areas within quantum communication and information technology

I am using this map to build breadth before selecting a tightly defined doctoral problem with a supervisor.

Information representation

State vectors and density matrices, discrete- and continuous-variable encodings, measurement, entanglement, and interfaces between flying and stationary qubits.

Quantum channel modeling

Mathematical noise maps and physical photon links, including loss, decoherence, atmospheric effects, detector behavior, channel capacity, and link-level simulation.

QKD and quantum cryptography

QKD uses a quantum channel and an authenticated classical channel to establish shared secret keys; it does not itself encrypt application data. I am studying protocol families, practical imperfections, finite-key security, authentication, and key-management integration.

Quantum networks and repeaters

Entanglement generation, swapping and purification, quantum memories, routing and scheduling, distributed protocols, network stacks, and hybrid classical control.

Quantum information and coding

Classical and quantum capacities, entanglement-assisted communication, quantum error correction, resource theories, and reliable transfer of quantum states.

Post-quantum and hybrid security

Post-quantum cryptography uses classical algorithms designed to resist quantum attacks; I am interested in how PQC, QKD, and conventional security can coexist in deployable networks.

Bridge from completed work

What my NOMA research contributes to this learning track

My thesis does not establish quantum-communication experience, and classical NOMA models do not transfer directly to quantum systems. What transfers is the discipline of stating assumptions, deriving measurable performance relationships, and validating models computationally.

Analytical performance modeling The methodology used to formulate BER, throughput, and error-propagation models provides a disciplined foundation for learning QBER and key-rate analysis.
Monte Carlo validation The habit of checking analytical predictions against simulation is relevant when learning photon loss, dark counts, channel noise, and finite-sample effects.
Receiver and channel thinking Detection, estimation, and communication-channel concepts provide a classical foundation for learning quantum measurement and noisy-channel models.
